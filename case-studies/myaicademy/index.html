How I thought about it

Pick a lens to see where each kind of thinking shaped the product. The page marks every moment, and you can step through them.

80%

80% of organisations that bought AI for everyone still report no material earnings impact. McKinsey, 2025, as quoted in the founder's pitch.

Licences bought. Work unchanged.

The training that came with the licences teaches the tool: what ChatGPT can do, how to write a prompt. It doesn't teach a finance lead to close the month faster, or a designer to brief Midjourney for a real campaign.

So people finish the course, go back to their desk and work the old way. For myAIcademy that means a learner who doesn't come back, and a company that doesn't renew.

Analytical thinking Framed with a market number, not an opinion: 80% of organisations see no earnings impact.

Product thinking Stated as a business risk, a learner who doesn't return and a company that doesn't renew, not as a missing feature.

Four decisions

Each started as a choice between options that all looked reasonable. Here is what I weighed and what it cost.

Ask how to begin, not who you are

Some people arrive knowing their job and nothing about AI. Others can't name the skills they want. One onboarding couldn't serve both.

  • Option AOne questionnaire for everyoneThorough, but slow for someone who already knows their role, and every extra question loses people.
  • Option BRole onlyFast, but a role says little about what you already know or want to reach.
  • ChosenTwo ways in, both ending with a planTell me your role reads the job and maps the skills; Answer a few questions walks through it step by step.

I let people choose their own speed into the product. The cost was designing and maintaining two routes, and the risk was people worrying they had picked the wrong one. That's what the line under the question is for: Two ways in. Both end with a plan made for you.

Product thinking Paid for a faster start with a second route to design and maintain.

Content thinking The reassurance line does the work a tooltip would otherwise do.

Two points on every skill, not one level

“Beginner” is true of most people for some AI skills and false for others. One level would have sent a designer who already uses Midjourney back to lesson one.

  • Option AOne overall levelQuick to answer, but it flattens uneven skills into one number.
  • Option BCurrent level per skillAccurate today, but it says nothing about how far the learner wants to go.
  • ChosenNow and goal on every skillTwo handles per slider: where you are and where you want to be. The gap becomes the path.

The gap between the two handles is what the path is built from, so the plan skips what you already know and stops where you want to stop. The trade-off is effort: four sliders ask more of you than one tap, so the list is cut to the four skills that matter for the role picked one step earlier.

Analytical thinking Replaced one self-reported level with a gap per skill that the path can measure.

Product thinking Cut the list to four skills per role, so accuracy didn't cost completion.

Aimy works inside the real tool

The positioning slide says it plainly: others teach the tool; myAIcademy proves the work got done. A chat window beside a lesson is still a classroom.

  • Option AA chatbot inside the lessonsFamiliar, but the learner still has to carry the answer across to the real tool.
  • Option BClick-by-click overlaysGets the task done, but nobody learns why, so they need the overlay next time too.
  • ChosenAn agent that works inside the real toolTell Aimy your goal; she turns it into a workflow and guides you inside the tool, with the exact prompts, actions and settings.

Aimy keeps both outcomes in view: the task gets finished, and each step says why, so next time you need her less. Let Aimy finish the rest is there for the day when shipping matters more than learning.

AI fluency Designed an agent that works inside the real tool, and decided where its autonomy stops.

Product thinking Positioned against course platforms, vendor academies and click-guides at once.

Before

Course platforms like Coursera and Udemy teach broadly. Vendor academies teach one tool. WalkMe and Whatfix guide clicks without teaching anything.

What a lesson should be

I owned the lesson content as well as the screens it sits in: the standard, the writing and the narration. Every lesson follows the same rules.

  • Named after an outcome, not a feature

    Turn market research into cited answers with NotebookLM, not Introduction to NotebookLM.

    Content thinking A title that names the result tells a learner why to start it.

  • Done in the real tool

    The learner works in the tool itself, with Aimy beside it giving the exact prompts, actions and settings.

    Product thinking Learning and doing happen in one place, so the lesson leaves real work behind.

  • Sized for a working day

    Paces from 10 to 60 minutes a day, so a lesson fits around the work people already do.

  • Organised in sections and levels

    The path is built from sections and three levels, Beginner, Intermediate and Advanced. The level comes from the learner's own skill gaps.

    Analytical thinking Levels come from the gap between Now and Goal on each skill, not from a single self-rating.

  • Current within 72 hours

    When a major tool changes, the affected lessons are refreshed within 72 hours.

    AI fluency Kept current by the lesson pipeline I built: scans pick the lesson, then screenshots, illustrations and an ElevenLabs voice are added.

The plainest one, plus a reason to keep going

The shipped screen is one centred component for phone and web: the badge, three short lines and a button. Inter Regular only, brand #3370FF, the title at 24px.

It kept Pure's restraint and swapped the stats for the one number that matters next: how many lessons until the next badge. It matches the streak and lesson-complete screens, so a reward never feels like a different app.

Colour

One blue does every job that matters: the selected card, the slider fill, the main button, the progress bar. Amber and yellow are kept for rewards, coins and streaks, so they never compete with an action.

Type: Inter

Inter Regular is used about 11,500 times in the file, Medium 4,900, Light and Semi Bold under 900 each, and the badge spec says Inter Regular only. Most text sits at 12 to 16px, so legibility at small sizes mattered more than personality.
