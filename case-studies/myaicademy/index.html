The brief

Companies bought AI licences for everyone. Almost nobody changed how they worked.

myAIcademy set out to be the adoption layer: the part between the licence and the finished work. Not another course library, and not a tour of buttons.

I joined to design the product and the brand from the ground up, across iOS, Android and web, with a founder and two engineers.

The job was to make one loop feel like one product: learn inside your role, practise on real work, and finish beside the tool your company already approves.

Bought for everyone, used by few

80%

of organisations report no material earnings impact from AI. One company in five sees any at all.

That's across more than 30 million Copilot seats alone, according to Microsoft and McKinsey in 2025.

The question in every room

“How does my team use this in its actual work?”

The founder had trained more than 10,000 professionals in person, and the same question came up every time. Employees knew the tool was there. They didn't know what to do inside it.

So the licence was never the hard part. The product had to start from the job, not from the tool.

How it works

Start with the job, not the tool.

A few questions about your role, and the path takes shape on the AI tools your organisation already approves. A consultant, a teacher and a lawyer never get the same course.

Then practise on real work.

Every lesson is a follow-along, done on the real tool, with each screen explained as it appears. When a tool ships a major release, the paths are rebuilt within 72 hours.

Finish beside the tool, with Aimy.

The lesson ends, the work doesn't. Aimy sits inside the approved tool, here ChatGPT's GPT builder, sets the outcome, follows the path and checks the work is finished. Every finished path adds a skill passport you can share.

What I owned

I designed myAIcademy end to end: the research synthesis, the flows, onboarding, the lesson player and Aimy, across iOS, Android and web.

I built one dark design system with a single accent in Figma and shipped it to all three platforms, then carried the same language into the identity, the website, the investor and sales decks, and social.

AI is in how I work, not only in what we teach. I prototype in code with Claude Code, make imagery with Higgsfield, voice lessons with ElevenLabs and run the content pipelines in n8n.

The part I'm proudest of is the loop feeling like one product, from the first question about your job to a finished, verified piece of work.
